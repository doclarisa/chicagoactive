# Too Cold to Walk? Chicagoland's Best Indoor and Mall Walking Spots

*November 1, 2026 · 6 min read · Active Chicagoland*

![Two smiling older women in fleece jackets walking briskly down a bright mall corridor](/blog/indoor-mall-walking-chicagoland-hero.png)

November in Chicago means wind that cuts through your coat, sidewalks that turn to ice overnight, and sunsets at 4:30.

It also means the end of your walking routine -- unless you take it **indoors.**

Mall walking is one of the most popular senior activities in America, and it's easy to see why. The floors are flat and smooth, the temperature stays comfortable, there are restrooms and benches everywhere, and there's coffee at the finish line. In Chicagoland, you also have indoor tracks, park district fieldhouses, and a couple of surprising options downtown.

Here's your guide to staying on your feet all winter.

## Why Mall Walking Works

- **No ice, no wind, no potholes.** Smooth, level floors are easier on your joints, and much safer for your balance, than an icy sidewalk.
- **It's free.** No membership or equipment. Just good shoes.
- **Restrooms, water, and benches are everywhere.** Rest whenever you want.
- **It's comfortable to walk alone.** Malls are well lit and staffed.
- **It's social.** Show up at the same time a few mornings in a row and you'll start seeing the same friendly faces. Many walkers say the friendships are the best part.

![A group of older adults laughing over coffee at a mall food court table after their walk](/blog/indoor-mall-walking-chicagoland-cafe.png)

## Where to Walk in Chicagoland

### Yorktown Center (Lombard)

Yorktown welcomes mall walkers **seven days a week, year-round**, and its doors open at **8 a.m.** (holiday hours may vary). The mall has a history of supporting walkers: a 2019 article on its website described a **Heart & Sole Walking Club**, launched with Humana, that had grown to about **700 members** and offered perks like store discounts and free walking gear.

**Before you go:** ask guest services whether the walking club is still active and which entrance to use.

📍 203 Yorktown Center, Lombard · [yorktowncenter.com](https://yorktowncenter.com/about/)

![An older man checking the step counter on his belt while walking through a sunny mall atrium](/blog/indoor-mall-walking-chicagoland-steps.png)

### Gurnee Mills (Gurnee)

If you want distance, this is the place. Gurnee Mills is **all on one level** -- no stairs, no escalators -- and its main corridor runs about **4,400 feet**, close to a mile from end to end. Walk the whole thing and back and you've covered **about 1.7 miles** without seeing a single snowflake.

**Tip:** mall hours vary by season, so check the mall's website and ask guest services whether walkers can come in before the stores open.

![An older couple holding hands as they walk away down a long, bright mall corridor](/blog/indoor-mall-walking-chicagoland-couple.png)

### Your Neighborhood Mall

Almost every Chicagoland mall welcomes walkers, and many open their doors before the stores do. Call guest services and ask three questions:

1. What time do the doors open for walkers?
2. Which entrance should I use?
3. Is there a walking club or a mapped route with distances?

You may find a whole walking community you didn't know existed.

## Indoor Tracks: Count Your Laps

If you like to know exactly how far you've gone, an indoor track is the answer. Several park districts open their tracks to the public in winter.

### Lincoln-Way Indoor Tracks (New Lenox area)

The New Lenox Park District opens the indoor tracks at **Lincoln-Way West** and **Lincoln-Way Central** high schools to adults 18 and older.

- **Free for Lincoln-Way residents**, $5 per visit for non-residents. Pick up a walking pass at the field house check-in desk.
- About **8 to 9 laps in lane 5 or 6 equals one mile.**
- **Fall season: September 9 – December 18.** Lincoln-Way West: Monday and Wednesday–Friday 6–9 p.m., Saturday 1–4 p.m., Sunday 9 a.m.–1 p.m. Lincoln-Way Central: Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday 6–9 p.m.
- Hours can end early on slow nights, and some dates are skipped, so check the calendar first.

[Details →](https://www.newlenoxparks.org/lincoln-way-central-west-indoor-track/)

### Stephen D. Persinger Recreation Center (Geneva)

This Geneva Park District center has a **three-lane indoor walking and running track**, and seniors get a real discount on a track-only pass:

- **Resident seniors:** $30 a year
- **Non-resident seniors:** $130 a year
- **Day visit:** $9 residents, $11 non-residents

[Details →](https://genevaparks.org/bestlife-fitness/stephen-d-persinger-recreation-center/indoor-track)

## Walking Clubs That Move Indoors

Some groups walk outside when the weather is nice and move indoors when it isn't, so you never have to give up your routine.

### Wood Dale Park District Senior Walking Club (55+)

The club walks the trails at **White Oaks Park** and **Recreation Park**. On bad-weather days, it moves to the **indoor track at the Recreation Complex** (111 E. Foster Ave.) -- and **coffee is available.** It's **$3** per session; register through the park district by the day before.

[Details →](https://www.active.com/wood-dale-il/walking/classes/senior-walking-club-2026-110510912)

## Chicago's Secret Indoor Walks

### The Chicago Pedway (Downtown)

Few Chicagoans realize there's a network of underground and enclosed walkways beneath the Loop. According to the City of Chicago, the Pedway runs about **five miles**, links **more than 40 blocks**, and connects **more than 50 buildings** -- shops, food courts, and train stations, all without stepping outside. Signs can be confusing, so bring a friend, download the city's Pedway map, and go on a weekday when the connecting buildings are open.

[City of Chicago Pedway page →](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/cdot/provdrs/ped/svcs/pedway.html)

### Garfield Park Conservatory (West Side)

Want to walk through a jungle in January? The conservatory's warm, green rooms are a treat in winter.

- **Admission:** free for Chicago residents (donations welcome; bring ID). Non-residents: $10 adults, **$5 seniors 65+**.
- **Reservations** are recommended to guarantee entry.
- **Hours:** closed Monday and Tuesday. Wednesday 10 a.m.–8 p.m.; Thursday–Sunday 10 a.m.–5 p.m.
- **Parking** is free in the visitor lot, and the CTA Green Line stops nearby.
- **Accessibility:** a limited number of free wheelchairs are available. Paths can be wet, and the Fern Room is not wheelchair accessible.

📍 300 N. Central Park Ave., Chicago · [garfieldconservatory.org](https://garfieldconservatory.org/faq)

## Tips for Happy Indoor Walking

- **Wear real walking shoes.** Mall floors are hard, so cushioning matters.
- **Go early.** Mornings are quiet and the corridors are wide open.
- **Bring a water bottle.** Indoor heating is drying.
- **Track your steps** with a pedometer or your phone. It's motivating to watch the numbers climb.
- **Find a buddy or a group.** You're far more likely to show up on a gray Tuesday if someone is waiting for you.
- **Use the CDC's free Mallwalking Guide** for tips on starting or joining a program: [read the guide →](https://www.cdc.gov/physicalactivity/downloads/mallwalking-guide.pdf)

## The Bottom Line

Winter doesn't have to stop you. Find a mall, a track, or a walking club, put on your sneakers, and keep moving until spring. You might even finish the season with a coffee crew and a few new friends.
