# 7 a.m., Pickleball, and Friends: Why Our Mornings in River Forest Are the Best Part of the Day

*November 1, 2026 · 5 min read · Active Chicagoland*

![Players at the net on the blue outdoor pickleball courts in River Forest, with autumn trees behind the fence](/blog/morning-pickleball-river-forest-hero.jpg)

If you pass the River Forest Park District courts early in the morning, you'll probably hear us before you see us. The pop of the paddles. Someone calling the score. And laughter -- a lot of laughter.

I started playing pickleball about two years ago. Some of my friends on the courts have been playing much longer. Most of us are seniors, and for a couple of hours in the morning, none of us are in a hurry to be anywhere else.

## How Our Mornings Work

In summer, we start around **7 a.m.** In the fall, when the mornings turn darker and cooler, we start around **7:30.** We play outside on the River Forest Park District courts whenever the weather lets us. When it rains or gets too cold, we move indoors to the **CRC -- the Community Recreation Center in Oak Park.**

We play -- but the playing is only half of it. We talk between games. We tease each other about missed shots. We laugh at ourselves. Somebody always has a story, and the coffee talk afterward is part of the ritual too.

![A player in a turquoise sweater hitting a forehand on the River Forest courts](/blog/morning-pickleball-river-forest-forehand.jpg)

## I Don't Make It Every Time -- and That's Okay

I'm a caregiver for my mother. Some mornings she has appointments, or my day is simply too full, and I can't go. But whenever I'm free, I'm there.

![The author's mother waving from a leaf-covered sidewalk on an autumn walk with the family dog](/blog/morning-pickleball-river-forest-mom-and-dog.jpg)

That's one of the things I love most about this group: nobody keeps score of who showed up. When you come back, you're simply welcomed back. For anyone juggling caregiving, work, or grandkids, a group like this fits into real life.

## More Than a Game

When I started, I thought I was joining a pickleball group. What I really joined was a group of friends.

Little by little, we got to know each other. When something good happens in someone's life, we're all happy for them. When something bad happens, we feel it too. And when someone is going through a difficult time, we try to help them get through it.

You can't plan a friendship like that. It grows one morning at a time -- between games and over coffee.

![Two friends hugging and laughing on the court, paddles in hand](/blog/morning-pickleball-river-forest-friends.jpg)

## Our Halloween Night at Robert's Westside

This fall, the group took the friendship off the courts. We went to a Halloween party together at **Robert's Westside**, a bar and music venue in Forest Park -- and yes, we dressed up.

We had a pirate (with a parrot on his shoulder), a fortune teller in a head scarf and layers of beads, an artist in a giant curly wig and a paint-splattered apron, an elegant lady in head-to-toe black, a cowboy with a bandana, and -- my favorite for a pickleball crowd -- someone who came as a golf ball.

Seeing everyone in costume, laughing just as hard as we do at 7:30 in the morning, reminded me that this group isn't really about pickleball. It's about each other.

![The pickleball group in Halloween costumes at Robert's Westside in Forest Park](/blog/morning-pickleball-river-forest-halloween-group.jpg)

## Why I'd Tell Anyone Over 50 to Try It

- **You don't need to be good.** We all started somewhere.
- **It's social from day one.** Pickleball is usually played as doubles -- you're never alone on the court.
- **It fits a busy life.** Come when you can.
- **Morning play sets up your whole day.** You're outside, moving, and laughing before most people finish breakfast.
- **The friendships are the real prize.**

**Want to try it?** Check the River Forest Park District and the Park District of Oak Park for open play times and beginner sessions.

## The Bottom Line

Come for the pickleball. Stay for the people.
